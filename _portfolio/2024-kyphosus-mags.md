I recovered MAGs from wild *Kyphosus* gut metagenomes and from bioreactors fed
macroalgae, and asked which microbes carry the enzymes to break down red, green
and brown algal polysaccharides. Few genomes can fully degrade any one sulfated
glycan on their own. The work is split across lineages, and many of the
enzymes are exported, which points to joint extracellular degradation.

## In the notebook

- Read-based taxonomy of the gut and bioreactor samples
- A PhyloPhlAn tree of the MAGs with rings for enzyme counts
- An interactive table of enzyme counts per MAG
- Short-chain fatty acid pathways
- How similar the enzymes are to GenBank, CAZy and SulfAtlas references
- A GH86 β-agarase/β-porphyranase gene tree
- Novelty of the biosynthetic gene clusters

The MAGs are on [Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.8277654).
