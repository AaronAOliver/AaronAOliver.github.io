mpCGCdb: polysaccharide-utilization gene clusters across the ocean

Preprint: Oliver, Podell & Allen 2026, bioRxiv Database: mpcgcdb.com Code: mpCGC pipeline Data: Zenodo

Bacteria break down polysaccharides with CAZyme gene clusters (CGCs): neighboring genes for glycoside hydrolases, lyases, sulfatases and transporters that act on one glycan together. Many marine glycans (carrageenan, fucoidan, ulvan) carry sulfate groups that must be removed before the sugars are usable, so marine CGCs often include sulfatases that CAZyme-only tools miss.

mpCGCdb is a web catalog of marine CGCs that I built from the Global Ocean Microbiome Catalog with my Nextflow pipeline, mpCGC. It is more than 15 times larger than the previous marine CGC resource, and it covers sulfatases and lineages beyond Bacteroidota. The figures below come from the preprint, and the interactive views come from mpcgcdb.com.

The mpCGC pipeline

Genes are predicted with Pyrodigal and annotated by DIAMOND and PyHMMER against dbCAN (CAZymes), SulfAtlas (sulfatases) and TCDB (transporters). A seed-and-extend algorithm then joins co-localized enzymes and transporters into clusters. The pipeline runs on conda, Docker, Singularity or Slurm, so the same workflow scaled to all 22,607 genomes. Its code is on GitHub.

GH13 (α-amylases), the multifunctional GH3 and GH23 (lytic transglycosylases) are the most common families in clusters. Among sulfatases, S1_6, S1_8 and S1_2 lead. More than 3,000 genomes carry over 25 CGCs each.

Undiscovered cluster diversity

Firmicutes, Bacteroidota and Planctomycetota hold the richest sets of cluster types, and Cyanobacteria, Desulfobacterota and Archaea the poorest. Chao2 estimates predict more than 9,000 distinct CAZyme and sulfatase arrangements in marine Bacteroidota and more than 4,000 in Planctomycetota. Current metagenomes capture under 40% of them.

Phylum-specific signatures

Phylum explains about 12% of the variation in cluster composition (PERMANOVA pseudo-F = 169.8, p < 0.001), but the groups overlap rather than separate cleanly. Every major phylum includes genomes with a large share of their genes in CGCs: 1,070 genomes have more than 10%. Many are uncharacterized strains, such as Cohnella sp. CIP 111063 (24.3%) and Sedimentisphaera salicampi (19.3%), which are candidates for cultivation.

The S1_4 sulfatase

Every enzyme family and subfamily in mpCGCdb has a page with these views. The interactive versions for S1_4 are below. They are copies of mpcgcdb.com/S1_4 as of October 2026. S1_4 occurs in clusters in 27.8% of Firmicutes and 22% of Bacteroidota genomes, but only 6.1% of Proteobacteria. Flavobacteriaceae form their own clade in the tree. The sulfatase colocalizes most often with S1_7, S1_16 and S1_8.

Enzyme colocalization

Families that share a substrate cluster together. The blocks along the diagonal match α-glucan, β-glucan, alginate and glycosaminoglycan, mannan, pectin, arabinan, xylan and fucan pathways. Lineages specialize: Bacteroidota in α- and β-glucans, Verrucomicrobiota and Planctomycetota in sulfated fucans, and Firmicutes in pectin. Several sulfatase subfamilies with no assigned EC number appear in these modules. S1_23, S1_24 and S1_28 likely act on sulfated fucans, while S1_30 and S1_56 sit with laminarin and alginate enzymes. These associations suggest substrates for uncharacterized sulfatases.
